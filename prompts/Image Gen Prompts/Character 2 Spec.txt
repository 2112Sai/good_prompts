Create a complete character-design reference sheet using BOTH attached files.

REFERENCE ROLES
- The character image is the approved design. It defines the character’s appearance, proportions, anatomy, colors, materials, accessories, and rendering style.
- The SVG defines the reference-sheet layout and panel organization.

This is a documentation and layout task—not another character redesign.

GOAL
Populate the template with consistent character artwork and useful reference details.

The character drawings will later be cropped and separated from their backgrounds for further processing. Prioritize clean silhouettes, plain white backgrounds, and separation between artwork and annotations.

GLOBAL PRESENTATION
- Solid white background (#FFFFFF) across the entire sheet.
- Black text.
- Thin black panel borders and dividers.
- White header strips.
- No blueprint styling.
- No background grid.
- No gradients, textures, scenery, or decorative background graphics.
- Keep the character artwork in its approved colors.
- Material swatches may use their actual reference colors.

CRITICAL EXTRACTION RULES
- Every character drawing must sit against uninterrupted solid white.
- No arrows, leader lines, pointers, annotation connectors, or floating callouts anywhere on the sheet.
- Do not draw lines from labels to body parts.
- No text or numbers over the character.
- No panel borders, dividers, rulers, or other graphics touching or crossing a character silhouette.
- Keep labels in separate caption areas below their drawings.
- Leave a clear white margin around every drawing, including accessories and protruding features.
- Open spaces between limbs and accessories must remain white.
- No ground planes, platforms, cast shadows, or shadow patches beneath the character.
- Preserve shading and self-shadowing ON the character so its forms remain three-dimensional.
- No checkerboard transparency patterns.
- Do not add a black outline around the character unless it already exists in the approved design.
- Preserve white or pale features belonging to the character; do not recolor them merely to contrast with the background.

CHARACTER AND STYLE CONSISTENCY
- Preserve the approved stylized Roblox game-model appearance.
- Match its chunky forms, simplified geometry, proportions, and silhouette.
- Preserve its exact number of limbs.
- Keep colors, accessories, markings, and intentional asymmetry consistent.
- Keep features on the same anatomical sides across all views.
- Do not revert to realistic fantasy illustration or detailed painted fur.
- Do not add unrelated equipment, new armor, weapons, ornaments, or anatomical features.
- For areas hidden in the reference, create a restrained, coherent continuation.
- Identify newly proposed details in a separate caption, never with a floating callout or arrow.

LAYOUT
Follow the attached SVG’s landscape composition and panel hierarchy:
- Large main-character panel on the left.
- Scale reference at the bottom left.
- Orthographic views in the upper center.
- Expressions/action poses and material colors below them.
- Faction context across the lower center.
- Twelve component-detail boxes in the upper right.
- Assembly key in the lower right.
- Identification header across the top.

Increase output resolution if needed while preserving the overall aspect ratio.

Populate every section as follows:

1. MAIN CHARACTER VIEW

Show one complete, full-body three-quarter view in a neutral stance.

- Preserve the approved design and rendering style.
- Show the entire silhouette without cropping.
- Make all limbs and accessories understandable.
- Keep the character centered with generous white space around it.
- No labels, measurements, lines, or graphics inside this artwork area.
- No ground shadow.

2. ORTHOGRAPHIC VIEWS — FRONT, SIDE, BACK

Draw the SAME character from the front, side, and back.

- Use matching scale, proportions, ground level, and neutral modeling pose.
- Avoid perspective distortion.
- Set neutral standing height to exactly 12.5 studs.
- Measure from the soles of the feet to the highest permanent anatomical point, including ears, horns, or equivalent features.
- Exclude removable accessories and temporary visual effects from the height measurement.
- Position limbs slightly away from the torso to clarify their shapes and attachments.
- Keep accessories and asymmetric features on the correct anatomical sides.
- Do not independently resize each view to fill its panel.
- Leave clear white space between the views.
- Do not draw alignment guides, rulers, dimension lines, or joint markers over or behind these drawings.

Place the labels in a separate caption row beneath the artwork:
“FRONT”
“SIDE”
“BACK”

3. EXPRESSIONS / ACTION POSES

Include separate compact studies of:
- Neutral or watchful expression.
- Angry or combat-ready expression.
- One attack wind-up or action pose appropriate to the character.

Keep the approved design consistent.

Do not invent weapons or abilities absent from the reference.

Keep each study isolated on white, with sufficient spacing to crop it independently.

Put any labels below each study. No arrows or floating annotations.

4. MATERIAL COLORS — EIGHT SWATCHES

Fill all eight swatch slots using the approved character palette.

Label each swatch with:
- Material or feature name.
- Hex color.
- Simple material treatment where useful.

Distinguish ordinary surface colors from emissive elements.

If fewer than eight materials exist, use supporting shades from the existing palette rather than introducing new colors.

Keep swatches and their labels completely separate from character drawings.

5. SCALE REFERENCE — ROBLOX STUDS

Use:
- Reference player: exactly 5 studs tall for this sheet, excluding hats and accessories.
- Boss: exactly 12.5 studs tall in its neutral standing pose.
- Height ratio: exactly 2.5×.

Draw the player and boss side by side at the same scale and ground level.

Keep both silhouettes clean and isolated on white.

Place these captions beneath the drawings:
“PLAYER — 5 STUDS”
“BOSS — 12.5 STUDS”
“HEIGHT RATIO — 2.5×”

A simple vertical ruler may appear in a separate strip beside the comparison, with 1-stud increments and a marked 12.5-stud endpoint.

The ruler must not touch either silhouette.
Do not use dimension arrows or extension lines.

Use studs for all dimensional information. Do not use meters or feet.

6. COMPONENTS / GEAR BREAKDOWN — TWELVE BOXES

Fill all twelve boxes with close-up drawings of actual features from the approved character.

Select useful subjects such as:
- Head and facial structure.
- Eyes.
- Masks or head accessories.
- Shoulder and limb connections.
- Hands, paws, or claws.
- Feet.
- Clothing or wrap construction.
- Markings or scars.
- Back forms.
- Fur clumps or hair masses.
- Material transitions.
- Signature accessories.

Adapt the subjects to the supplied character. Do not invent features merely to fill the boxes.

For every box:
- Show one clearly isolated detail or a closely related set of details.
- Use a plain white background.
- Keep the drawing away from the panel border.
- Put a short descriptive caption below it.
- Do not use arrows, leader lines, pointers, or text over the artwork.

If needed, identify anatomical left or right in the caption.

7. ASSEMBLY KEY — CLEAN CONSTRUCTION STUDIES

Explain the major body regions and separate accessories through isolated drawings.

- Use a main construction study plus separate supporting component studies.
- Keep each drawing on white with space around it.
- Use captions beneath drawings to identify body regions or accessories.
- Show attachment surfaces through the drawing angle or an additional isolated view—not through arrows or connector lines.
- Do not overlay skeletons, joint dots, wireframes, labels, or numbers on the main character.
- Do not imply organic body parts are mechanically detachable.
- Do not invent missing accessories or components.
- Keep any conceptual modeling or rigging notes in a separate text area.

If numbered identification is necessary, place the number beside the caption, not on the component.

8. FACTION CONTEXT

Use supplied faction information if available.

If none is supplied:
- Keep “FACTION: TBD.”
- Use a short separate text block marked “CONTEXT: TBD.”
- Do not add an environment illustration, scenery, or decorative vignette.
- Do not invent established lore.

Keep this panel visually simple and separate from the character artwork.

9. HEADER

Use:
- Working title: short descriptive identifier based on the character.
- Role: “BOSS”
- Faction: “TBD” unless provided.
- Levels: “TBD” unless provided.
- Sheet: “CHARACTER REFERENCE”
- Height: “12.5 STUDS”
- Scale reference: “2.5× A 5-STUD PLAYER”

Keep all header text black on white.

FINAL CHECK
Verify:
- The approved character design is preserved.
- Every view has consistent anatomy and proportions.
- All required panels are populated.
- The main and orthographic character drawings have clean, unobstructed silhouettes.
- Backgrounds are pure white.
- There are no arrows, leader lines, floating callouts, or annotation connectors.
- No labels, borders, rulers, or other graphics touch the character.
- There are no ground shadows or background patterns.
- Labels sit in separate caption areas.
- The boss is 12.5 studs tall in the neutral standing views.
- The reference player is 5 studs tall.
- All small details remain readable.

DELIVERABLE
Produce the completed visual reference sheet—not a written plan.

Also provide the main character view and the front, side, and back views as separate, full-resolution images if supported. Each separate image should contain only one character view on solid white, without labels, borders, rulers, or other graphics.

If separate image output is not supported, ensure each view occupies a clean rectangular area that can be cropped without including text or panel borders.

Prioritize faithful character documentation and easy artwork extraction over decorative presentation.
