Create a complete item-design reference sheet using BOTH attached files.

REFERENCE ROLES
- The item image is the approved design. It defines the item’s appearance, proportions, geometry, colors, materials, markings, components, and rendering style.
- The SVG defines the reference-sheet layout and panel organization.

This is a documentation and layout task—not an item redesign.

GOAL
Populate the template with consistent item artwork and useful reference details.

The item drawings will later be cropped and separated from their backgrounds for further processing. Prioritize clean silhouettes, plain white backgrounds, and separation between artwork and annotations.

GLOBAL PRESENTATION
- Solid white background (#FFFFFF) across the entire sheet.
- Black text.
- Thin black panel borders and dividers.
- White header strips.
- No blueprint styling.
- No background grid.
- No gradients, textures, scenery, or decorative graphics in the background.
- Keep the item artwork in its approved colors.
- Preserve material textures and surface treatments that belong to the approved item.
- Material swatches may use their actual reference colors.

CRITICAL EXTRACTION RULES
- Every item drawing must sit against uninterrupted solid white.
- No arrows, leader lines, pointers, annotation connectors, or floating callouts anywhere on the sheet.
- Do not draw lines from labels to item features.
- No text or numbers over the item, except markings already present in the approved design.
- No panel borders, dividers, rulers, or other graphics touching or crossing an item silhouette.
- Keep labels in separate caption areas below their drawings.
- Leave a clear white margin around every drawing, including protruding features and attached components.
- Open spaces, holes, handles, and gaps between components must remain white wherever the background is visible through them.
- No ground planes, platforms, cast shadows, or shadow patches beneath the item.
- Preserve shading and self-shadowing ON the item so its forms remain three-dimensional.
- No checkerboard transparency patterns.
- Do not add a black outline around the item unless it already exists in the approved design.
- Preserve white, pale, or transparent features belonging to the item; do not recolor them merely to contrast with the background.

ITEM AND STYLE CONSISTENCY
- Preserve the approved stylized Roblox game-asset appearance.
- Match its chunky forms, simplified geometry, proportions, and silhouette.
- Preserve its exact number of parts, attachments, openings, and repeated features wherever established by the reference.
- Keep colors, materials, markings, and intentional asymmetry consistent.
- Keep features on the same physical sides across all views.
- Do not mirror asymmetric details to make the layout more convenient.
- Do not revert to realistic product photography, realistic fantasy illustration, or excessively detailed painted rendering.
- Do not add unrelated attachments, ornaments, mechanisms, effects, or components.
- Do not add glow, particles, or emissive elements unless they exist in the approved design.
- For areas hidden in the reference, create a restrained, coherent continuation.
- Identify newly proposed hidden-surface details in a separate caption, never with a floating callout or arrow.
- Use a consistent item orientation throughout the sheet. Define “front” from the item’s primary functional or presentation face. If ambiguous, choose a clear orientation and explain it briefly in a separate text area.

LAYOUT
Follow the attached SVG’s landscape composition and panel hierarchy. Preserve its overall arrangement, adapting the section titles and contents for an item:
- Large main-item panel on the left.
- Scale reference at the bottom left.
- Orthographic views in the upper center.
- Functional views / alternate states and material colors below them.
- Faction / item context across the lower center.
- Twelve component-detail boxes in the upper right.
- Assembly / construction key in the lower right.
- Identification header across the top.

Increase output resolution if needed while preserving the overall aspect ratio.

Populate every section as follows:

1. MAIN ITEM VIEW

Show one complete three-quarter view of the item in its default state.

- Preserve the approved design and rendering style.
- Show the entire silhouette without cropping.
- Choose an angle that makes the item’s overall form, depth, and major features understandable.
- Keep attached components visible or spatially understandable.
- Keep the item centered with generous white space around it.
- No labels, measurements, lines, or graphics inside this artwork area.
- No ground shadow.
- Do not add a stand, mount, or support unless it belongs to the approved design.

2. ORTHOGRAPHIC VIEWS — FRONT, SIDE, BACK

Draw the SAME item from the front, side, and back.

- Use matching scale, proportions, orientation, and baseline.
- Avoid perspective distortion.
- Show the item in the same default configuration in every view.
- Do not independently resize each view to fill its panel.
- Keep asymmetric details and attachments on the correct physical sides.
- Leave clear white space between views.
- Do not draw alignment guides, rulers, dimension lines, or markers over or behind these drawings.
- If an important feature is hidden in these three views, show it in a component-detail box or functional study rather than changing the template’s main panel hierarchy.

Place the labels in a separate caption row beneath the artwork:
“FRONT”
“SIDE”
“BACK”

DIMENSION CONVENTION
- Use supplied dimensions if available.
- Use studs for all dimensional information.
- If no dimensions are supplied, assign one consistent proposed overall size and clearly label it “PROPOSED SCALE” in a separate text area.
- Do not present an estimated dimension as an approved specification.
- Use that same size consistently across the orthographic views, scale comparison, and header.
- Define overall dimensions as height × width × depth in the item’s default orientation.
- Include all permanent protrusions in those dimensions.
- Measure removable components in the default attached configuration, unless a separate caption explicitly identifies a different state.

3. FUNCTIONAL VIEWS / ALTERNATE STATES

Replace the character expression/action section with three separate compact item studies:
- An alternate three-quarter angle showing features not clearly visible in the main view.
- A functional or interaction-oriented view showing the item’s primary usable surface, grip, opening, attachment area, or contact point.
- An alternate state, only if supported by the approved design—for example, open/closed, folded/unfolded, or attached/detached.

If the item has no supported moving parts or alternate states, use a top view, underside view, or additional useful angle instead.

- Do not invent articulation, moving mechanisms, hidden compartments, abilities, or effects.
- Do not add a character, hand, or user to these studies unless explicitly supplied or requested.
- Keep the approved design consistent.
- Keep each study isolated on white, with sufficient spacing to crop it independently.
- Put labels below each study.
- No arrows, floating annotations, motion trails, or explanatory graphics over the artwork.

4. MATERIAL COLORS — EIGHT SWATCHES

Fill all eight swatch slots using the approved item palette.

Label each swatch with:
- Material or feature name.
- Hex color.
- Simple material treatment where useful, such as matte, satin, polished, translucent, or emissive.

Additional requirements:
- Distinguish ordinary surface colors from emissive elements.
- Only label a material emissive if the approved design supports it.
- If fewer than eight materials exist, use supporting shades from the existing palette rather than introducing new colors.
- Treat lighting highlights and shadows as supporting shades, not automatically as separate physical materials.
- Keep swatches and their labels completely separate from item drawings.
- Where exact source colors cannot be determined, identify hex values as approximate reference colors.

5. SCALE REFERENCE — ROBLOX STUDS

Use:
- Reference player: exactly 5 studs tall for this sheet, excluding hats and accessories.
- Item: the supplied approved dimensions, or the clearly identified proposed dimensions established for this sheet.
- Scale relationship: calculated consistently from those dimensions.

Draw a simple neutral Roblox reference player and the item side by side at the same scale.

- Keep both silhouettes clean and isolated on white.
- Use a shared baseline without drawing a baseline or ground plane.
- Show the item in its default orientation.
- If the item would normally be held, worn, or suspended, show it independently beside the player rather than adding invented mounting hardware.
- Do not distort or enlarge a small item merely to fill the panel.
- If a small item needs an enlarged study for readability, place it in a separate detail box and label that study “ENLARGED DETAIL” beneath the drawing.

Place these captions beneath the drawings, replacing bracketed fields with the actual values:
“PLAYER — 5 STUDS”
“ITEM — [HEIGHT] H × [WIDTH] W × [DEPTH] D STUDS”
“HEIGHT RATIO — [ITEM HEIGHT ÷ 5]× PLAYER HEIGHT”

If dimensions are not supplied, also include:
“PROPOSED SCALE — NOT AN APPROVED DIMENSION”

A simple vertical ruler may appear in a separate strip beside the comparison.
- Use clearly labeled stud increments appropriate to the item’s size.
- The ruler must not touch either silhouette.
- Do not use dimension arrows or extension lines.
- Do not use meters, feet, or inches.

6. COMPONENTS / FEATURE BREAKDOWN — TWELVE BOXES

Fill all twelve boxes with close-up drawings of actual features from the approved item.

Select useful subjects appropriate to the item, such as:
- Primary body or housing.
- Front face or functional end.
- Rear face.
- Top surface.
- Underside.
- Handle, grip, or contact surface.
- Existing attachment or mounting point.
- Existing joint, hinge, seam, or connection.
- Openings, cutouts, or recesses.
- Markings, symbols, or decorative details.
- Material transitions.
- Signature component or accessory.

Adapt the subjects to the supplied item. Do not invent features merely to fill the boxes.

For every box:
- Show one clearly isolated detail or a closely related set of details.
- Use a plain white background.
- Keep the drawing away from the panel border.
- Put a short descriptive caption below it.
- Do not use arrows, leader lines, pointers, or text over the artwork.
- Identify left/right, front/rear, or top/underside in the caption where useful.
- Keep attached features attached in close-up studies unless the reference clearly supports their removal.
- A close-up may crop surrounding item geometry, but keep the featured detail fully visible and separated from panel borders by white space.

If the item has fewer than twelve distinct components, use alternate angles, surface close-ups, or construction details of existing features. Do not create twelve fictional parts.

7. ASSEMBLY / CONSTRUCTION KEY — CLEAN CONSTRUCTION STUDIES

Explain the item’s major forms and any genuinely separate components through isolated drawings.

- Use a main construction study plus separate supporting component studies.
- Keep each drawing on white with space around it.
- Use captions beneath drawings to identify major forms, components, or accessories.
- Show attachment surfaces through the drawing angle or an additional isolated view—not through arrows or connector lines.
- Do not overlay wireframes, joint dots, labels, numbers, or technical diagrams on the main item.
- Do not imply that continuous or fused surfaces are detachable.
- Do not invent internal mechanisms, fasteners, sockets, or hidden components.
- Show a disassembled or exploded arrangement only when the approved reference clearly supports those separate parts.
- Even in a supported exploded arrangement, do not use connector lines, arrows, or graphics between components.
- If the item is a single continuous object, use whole-item construction angles and surface-region studies instead of a fictional assembly breakdown.
- Keep conceptual modeling, pivot, or implementation notes in a separate text area.
- Distinguish proposed implementation notes from visible approved design facts.

If numbered identification is necessary, place the number beside the caption, not on the component.

8. FACTION / ITEM CONTEXT

Use supplied faction, ownership, item category, and gameplay information if available.

If none is supplied:
- Keep “FACTION: TBD.”
- Use a short separate text block marked “CONTEXT: TBD.”
- Use “FUNCTION: TBD” if the item’s purpose is not established.
- Do not invent established lore, rarity, stats, abilities, damage values, or acquisition methods.
- Do not add an environment illustration, scenery, or decorative vignette.

Keep this panel visually simple and separate from the item artwork.

9. HEADER

Use:
- Working title: short descriptive identifier based on the item.
- Type: supplied item category, or “ITEM” if unspecified.
- Faction: “TBD” unless provided.
- Levels: “TBD” unless provided.
- Sheet: “ITEM REFERENCE.”
- Dimensions: “[HEIGHT] H × [WIDTH] W × [DEPTH] D STUDS.”
- Scale reference: “5-STUD PLAYER.”
- Dimension status: “APPROVED DIMENSIONS” only if supplied; otherwise “PROPOSED SCALE.”

Replace bracketed fields with the actual values used on the sheet.
Keep all header text black on white.

FINAL CHECK
Verify:
- The approved item design is preserved.
- Every view has consistent geometry, proportions, materials, markings, and component placement.
- All required panels are populated.
- The main and orthographic drawings have clean, unobstructed silhouettes.
- Backgrounds are pure white.
- There are no arrows, leader lines, floating callouts, or annotation connectors.
- No labels, borders, rulers, or other graphics touch the item.
- There are no ground shadows or background patterns.
- Labels sit in separate caption areas.
- All orthographic views use the same scale and default configuration.
- Dimensions are consistent across the header, orthographic section, and scale comparison.
- The reference player is exactly 5 studs tall.
- The item and player are depicted at the same scale in the scale-reference panel.
- Any unsupplied dimensions are clearly marked as proposed.
- No unsupported moving parts, internal mechanisms, accessories, or functions have been invented.
- All small details remain readable.

DELIVERABLE
Produce the completed visual reference sheet—not a written plan.

Also provide the main item view and the front, side, and back views as separate, full-resolution images if supported. Each separate image should contain only one item view on solid white, without labels, borders, rulers, or other graphics.

If separate image output is not supported, ensure each view occupies a clean rectangular area that can be cropped without including text or panel borders.

Prioritize faithful item documentation and easy artwork extraction over decorative presentation.
